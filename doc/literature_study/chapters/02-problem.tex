\chapter{Problem description}
\label{problem}
Speech itself is a complex and continuous valued analogue signal, so that processing in digitalized form is non-trivial and requires multiple (pre-)processing steps.
Typically the majority of these steps are performed using either mathematical or neural network models, that are learned based on some kind of training data in a supervised manner.

The process of producing and labeling high quality datasets is time- and cost-intensive. 
Usually it is therefore impossible to create and train a completely optimized model for a given taks and rather already existing general systems are used and only fine-tuned for specified solutions.

This is also the case for this project, because the dataset contains only 6000 training samples with around 10.5 hours of speech. 
Nowadays such models typically have several hundred million to a few billion parameter and require 1000 to 10000 hours of transcribed speech to achieve good results.
On the other hand 10 to 100 hours can be enough to adapt respectively fine-tune it to a new language, because most of the needed feature and mapping functionalities stay the same.

The advantage of Esperanto in this case is the history of the language itself.
Many words are similar to western european languages like french, english, german and latin.
Others parts are heavily influenced by slawic langugaes like russian and polnish, because of the roots of the inventor.
So a big part if the sounds and their according transcriptions can be assumed to be quiet similar to these languages, for which a lot of training data is publicely available.
Most of the commercial and popular open-source models should therefore be already good in transcribing the basics of Esperanto. 

Additionally, the grammatical rules and orthography of Esperanto are by purpose relatively simple.
Back then one design goal was to keep it simple to learn and resulted in a language which is phonematic, meaning that it has a bijective matching between phonemes and symbols, made out of latin symbols with additional diacritic extensions.

Particullary these special symbols can be a problem for the models because they are not part of the original training data. 
Also the very limited training data should not be iterated to often to prevent overfitting of the models, what would reduce the quality again.

Speech recognition itself is complicated not only because of the variety of languages, but also because of the quality of the recorded audio. 
Normal usage scenarios for speech recognition systems barely include a perfectly controlled, quiet environment with high quality recordings but rather multiple speakers, a wide variety of background noises and low-quality recoring devices.
So a fully functioning ASR system must include preporcessing steps to miniimize such effects. 
This can be done deterministically with classical signal processing techniques or again with end-to-end systems (neural networks).
Achieving a sufficient performance in arbitrary environments therefore requires even more data samples to cover as much of these additional effects as possible.
